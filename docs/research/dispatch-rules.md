**7.1c A dispatch is one step, not a shift.** Size each dispatch so the agent finishes it in a fresh context with room left to debug its own work. A brief long enough to need more than ten minutes of reading and doing is a plan, not a step: cut it into steps, dispatch them in sequence, and give each brief only its step plus whatever earlier steps returned that it needs. A new step goes to a fresh agent; 7.5 covers follow-ups on the step an agent already holds.
**7.1f An overloaded brief is refused, not ground through.** Every dispatched agent, `lead` or worker, checks its brief before any work and refuses it when any of these holds: more than one done-check or more than one commit; work it expects to overrun its own 7.1d cap; more than about a page of brief, or more than five files in scope; a mandated heavy run — full suite, mutation run, whole-tree gate — that alone would take most of the cap; or a ruling it needs and does not have. A refusal returns at once with no edits made: a first line `OVERLOADED: <the test failed, with its numbers>`, then a proposed split into steps that each pass this check, each with its done-check. An agent that finds partway that its step is bigger than its brief said stops at the next safe point and reports what is done, and what remains as a proposed split in the same shape, rather than push on to the cap. A `lead` refuses the same way for a step it was about to dispatch and cannot cut itself. The dispatcher runs this check on its own brief before sending it. A refused brief is re-cut into the proposed steps, or the dispatcher's own better split, and dispatched again; only the owner, in their own words, orders an agent to take an oversized step anyway. A refusal is not a failure and is not counted against the agent.
